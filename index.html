2026 Full-stack AI platform

NAFTAL — Espace IA Interne

A role-scoped internal AI platform for Naftal, Algeria's national fuel distributor. Employees sign in, the backend resolves their role, district and centre, and a fully local model produces grounded, cited French reports from the company's own operational data — nothing ever leaves the machine.

  • The permission boundary is the product. A dedicated security test suite runs against the real database and proves that no user — and no prompt-injected instruction to the AI — can read data outside their own district or centre.
  • Grounded, not merely fluent. The agent calls tools for real figures and cites them, rather than narrating plausible-sounding numbers.
  • A whole product, not a notebook. Auth with OTP, brute-force lockout, password reset, an admin account lifecycle in which an applicant never chooses their own scope, analytics with PDF export, and 57 tests.
  • About the public demo: it is the real frontend on synthetic data, with scope filtering applied per request so the isolation can be seen by switching accounts. A 14B-parameter local model will not run on free hosting, so the assistant's prose is pre-written there — the figures it quotes are still computed from the seeded data.

2026 LLM system design

Algerian Coast Marine Weather Bot

A Telegram bot that rates conditions for sailing, fishing and swimming across six cities on the Algerian Mediterranean coast, every morning at 07:00. Marine forecasts are easy to find and hard to read — “1.2 m at 4 s” and “1.2 m at 9 s” are the same number and completely different days on the water.

  • Code decides, the model writes. Every rating comes from plain JavaScript over the forecast data. The LLM receives an assessment that is already final and turns it into readable prose — it cannot change a rating, because the ratings are re-stamped from the deterministic assessment after the response is parsed.
  • Four gates on the model's output, including numeric traceability: any number in the prose that matches no real measurement rejects the response. Failing a gate costs nothing, because the deterministic report was rendered before the model was called.
  • One model's number is not the answer. Asked about the same hour at the same point, four wave models disagreed by 0.62 m at worst — against a swimming caution band 0.50 m wide. The bot combines curated models in whichever direction is conservative for each variable, and says so when they disagree.
  • Measuring it found two real bugs. Combining WMO weather codes by max ranks snow above heavy rain and lets a single model outvote the rest — one model produced 32 of 33 forecast thunderstorm hours. Together with a spread computed over the wrong hours, these had the bot rating AVOID on 67–72% of samples. A report that never says yes gets ignored, which is a safety failure of its own.

2026 Symbolic AI

Car Diagnosis Expert System

A rule-based expert system that diagnoses car faults by asking the questions a mechanic would. A forward-chaining inference engine walks a JSON rule base, choosing what to ask next from what it has already established.

  • Knowledge separated from code. Rules and questions live in JSON, so the knowledge base can grow without anyone touching the engine.
  • Explainable by construction. Every conclusion traces back through the rules that fired — the opposite of a black box.
  • Python
  • Forward chaining
  • JSON rule base
  • Tkinter

2025 Deep learning

Moths vs. Butterflies — CNN Image Classifier

A convolutional neural network that separates two visually similar classes, trained on a Kaggle set of 40 butterfly and moth species relabelled for binary classification. Built during my third year of bachelor studies.

  • The interesting part is the class boundary. Moths and butterflies are close enough that the network has to learn wing posture and texture, not just colour.
  • Preprocessing, normalisation and data augmentation; binary cross-entropy with Adam; stable training and validation performance.

About

I'm Mohamed Amar Selmani, a computer science graduate of Saad Dahlab University – Blida 1.

What runs through my projects is a preference for systems that know the limits of their own evidence. The marine bot refuses to say “safe” in any language, and reports an unchecked warning source as unchecked rather than as clear. The Naftal platform proves its permission boundary in a test suite instead of asserting it in a README. The expert system can show you exactly which rules produced its answer. I would rather ship something that says “I don't know” correctly than something that is confidently wrong.

I also write things down. Each repository documents not only what the system does, but what it does not establish — usually the more useful half.
